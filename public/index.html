The standard dose is the starting point; with exploring on, the app tries up and down in unit-dose steps. Turn it off to always use exactly the standard dose. If you name the unit (e.g. “pill”), doses show as counts — 144 mg with a 48 mg unit dose displays as “3 pills”.

Each night the app picks one option. Options keep their own history — renaming an option keeps its history; a removed option's past nights still count.

Occasionally suggests a “skip” control night, so the report can show the average effect of doing any of these options versus nothing — on both HRV and resting HR.

Your belief, before your own data, that this helps your HRV. 50% = no prior. Higher makes the scheduler try it more often early — this is exactly the chance it's scheduled before any nights are logged. Capped at 80% so it still gets enough OFF nights to actually measure. It never changes the measured efficacy numbers and fades as real nights accumulate.

💍 Oura sync

One-time setup: sign in at cloud.ouraring.com → Developer → OAuth applications and register a private app with redirect URI http://localhost:8777/oura/callback. Paste its Client ID and Client Secret here, then connect. After that, opening Sleep Bandit auto-fills each morning's HRV (averaged from Oura's raw 5-minute samples, one decimal more precise than the Oura app shows) and lowest resting heart rate (min of the same 5-minute samples the Oura app displays; the API's 30-second-based low is stored alongside and appears in the export). Oura's number replaces a hand-typed one when they differ; every change is kept in the audit log.
